## Primary-source reading notes

Every source below was inspected on **2026-10-31**. These notes distinguish a documented capability from my inference about the remaining delta. Search snippets served only to locate material; technical comparisons below use opened source text. Sources were read, not installed or executed. Absence from an inspected section is not proof of absence from the entire project.

### S1 — rounded-publication reconstruction is established

**Mathew Abado and George Stefan, _Reconstruction attack risk using Statistics Canada Census Data_, released 2024-03-25.** [Primary PDF](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-522-x/2022001/article/00008-eng.pdf), especially §4.1–4.3, PDF pp. 4–6.

The study turns rounded contingency-table information into constraints and uses Sugar/PicoSAT to obtain compatible microdata. It explicitly models count uncertainty with inequalities and uncertain population size. This defeats any claim that interpreting rounded tables as feasible integer worlds is new. The inspected workflow studies reconstruction risk rather than a comparison-specific minimum disclosure request.

### S2 — arithmetic feasibility of rounded summaries already ships

**scrutiny, Lukas Jung.** [Maintainer vignette](https://lhdjung.github.io/scrutiny/articles/grim.html) and [primary `R/grim.R`](https://github.com/lhdjung/scrutiny/blob/main/R/grim.R). Read the input/rounding explanation and the complete `grim_scalar` implementation.

The code preserves printed precision through strings, handles percentages, reconstructs neighboring integer totals, rerounds, and checks consistency. Our tool must not present those checks as an invention. Its intended extra contract combines multiple constraints with a comparison and a minimum disclosure set; the inspected function implements single-summary consistency.

### S3 — a direct public-facing consistency product exists

**ABC Tools GRIM & GRIMMER.** [Live tool page](https://abc.tools/stats/grim-test/) and [operator's guide](https://abc.tools/stats/grim-test/guide/). Read the form, result semantics, and guide's limitations.

The public interface accepts a reported mean, optional SD, sample size, and items per participant. It reports arithmetic consistency. I inspected the page/documentation, not the JavaScript runtime. The documented interface does not offer the joint verdict/disclosure contract proposed here.

### S4 — the minimum-disclosure mathematics is a decision reduct

**Henzel, Janusz, Sikora and Ślęzak, _On Positive-Correlation-Promoting Reducts_, 2020.** [Authored PDF mirror](https://scispace.com/pdf/on-positive-correlation-promoting-reducts-199tw8gtaj.pdf), §2, pp. 215–216; [RoughSets primary source](https://github.com/janusza/RoughSets/blob/master/R/FeatureSelection.R#L1491), lines 1491–1563.

The paper explains ordinary decision reducts before its own extension. RoughSets exposes an all-reducts function. Our feasible worlds can be rows, counts can be attributes, and signs can be decisions: taking a smallest returned reduct solves our selection problem. That mapping is my inference and makes the algorithmic overlap explicit. No new reduct theory is claimed. What remains proposed is constructing this decision table from a rounded report and checking the whole transformation independently.

### S5 — selecting facts to inspect for fact-checking is also established

**Sintos, Agarwal and Yang, _Selecting Data to Clean for Fact Checking: Minimizing Uncertainty vs. Maximizing Surprise_, PVLDB 2019.** [Primary PDF](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol12/p2408-sintos.pdf). Read the introduction, §2.1's model and Eq. (1), and §3.3–3.4.

Their model reveals selected numerical values at a cost and optimizes expected residual variance or counterargument probability. It allows a joint distribution; independence is an assumption for particular results, not the whole formulation.

**My reduction:** assign positive mass to every feasible integer table and use its comparison sign as f. Expected conditional variance is zero exactly when each disclosure-answer class has a single sign. Searching budgets therefore contains our mathematical target. “We use guarantees instead of probabilities” is not a sufficient novelty defense. I did not find the rounded-input plus independent-certificate pipeline in the inspected sections.

### S6 — uncertainty-aware analysis and targeted cleaning have a product workflow

**Mimir / ODIn Lab.** [Primary project overview](https://odin.cse.buffalo.edu/research/mimir/), sections LOAD, PLOT and ANALYZE.

Mimir tracks uncertain results and points users toward relevant cleaning work. This is a close workflow neighbour. The overview does not establish the particular exact minimality contract. The linked [SQL/ANALYZE documentation](https://git.odin.cse.buffalo.edu/Mimir/mimir-cli/wiki/Mimir-SQL) returned 403, so a detailed capability comparison remains limited.

### S7 — query determinacy and exposing only useful information are old goals

**Benedikt, Bourhis, Jachiet and Tsamoura, _Balancing Expressiveness and Inexpressiveness in View Design_, KR 2020.** [Primary proceedings PDF](https://proceedings.kr.org/2020/12/kr2020-0012-benedikt-et-al.pdf), introduction and formal definitions.

The paper studies distributed views that support utility queries while limiting disclosure, including a minimal-information requirement. It is a conceptual ancestor, not an inspected implementation of the rounded-count audit. Our restriction to choosing existing individual counts on one bounded publication instance is narrower than general view design, and does not make query determinacy new.

### S8 — patents already cover choosing cleaning objects for uncertain queries

**CN106897375A, Zhejiang University, published 2017-06-27.** [Primary patent text](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN106897375A/en). Read the description's algorithm and claims 1–5, using the displayed Chinese text and English translation.

It selects uncertain objects to clean under a resource budget, uses a joint-entropy quality objective, and describes exact and approximate search. This is substantial conceptual overlap. A zero-entropy specialization may also express a decisive disclosure set. The inspected claims do not describe the complete rounded-table certificate pipeline. This is a technical reading of the publication, not an opinion on enforceability or freedom to operate.

## Claim revision after the search

The committed longlist's first claim proposed rounded-table comparison checking plus the smallest decisive disclosure set. After S1–S5 and S8, I explicitly withdrew any implied claim to new reconstruction, a new selection problem, or a new selection algorithm. The finalist fixes the input semantics, three-way verdict, nonadaptive universal quantifier, and end-to-end replay requirement. The narrower candidate is a practical combination of established parts. The original longlist and every query remain intact; no failed search was relabeled as successful.

The strongest attack is to connect an existing rounded-table feasibility engine to RoughSets, or specialize the S5/S8 objectives to zero uncertainty. That nearly supplies the mathematics already. The candidate survives only if the complete audit artifact and workflow are both useful and not already implemented; merely naming the combination differently would not suffice.

## Verdict on novelty

**Provisional, modest integration novelty; substantial prior-art risk.** I did not find a primary source I could inspect that implements the whole claim, but I cannot infer worldwide absence from these searches. Reconstruction, exact arithmetic, query determinacy, uncertainty-directed cleaning, decision reducts, and independent checking are established. The plausible remaining contribution is making a rounded public comparison produce both checkable opposing worlds and a provably minimum fixed request for existing source counts in one bounded, independently verified workflow. U1 and inaccessible deeper Mimir docs limit confidence. I would submit this as a falsifiable finalist for Challenge, not describe it as a new mathematical discovery or a strong novelty win.
