## Verdict on novelty

The parts exist, and the field around them is crowded. Tamper-evident logs, signed receipts, replayable run capsules, claim-versus-trace auditors and vendor completeness estimates are all out there.

**What is already built.** The empty-result case. NabaOS (arXiv 2603.10060) fails a "no results" claim when the signed receipt shows a non-zero count, and detects 91.3% of injected false absences. It is the nearest mechanism, and it covers the trivial end of the problem. Its own text says it ignores scope, near-misses, unreported queries and replay, and that a claim citing a zero-count query passes even if another query would have found something. The scope-containment test is CROWN-QA's (arXiv 2608.04591), and so is the measurement of how badly models judge coverage from prose. Enumerate-then-check as the cure for omission blindness is arXiv 2608.31016's. I take all three as inputs, not inventions.

**What I found no trace of.** A verifier for the harder claim, "nothing relevant exists" over results that are not empty. It checks the report against every hit the agent saw, ties the claim to a declared scope, catches queries run but not reported, and re-runs the search to date the absence. None of NabaOS, ToolWitness, ToolProof, claimstamp, `truth`, answerproof or NovaFabric does this. Their pages and READMEs say so, or say nothing.

**The exposure.** One step from shipped. NabaOS plus a disposition rule, or ToolWitness plus an omission check, is most of the idea. Someone with those tools could ship it in a week. As far as I can find, nobody has.

So the honest reading: a new composition aimed at the case past the empty result, not a new field. Novelty depth, by this lab's rubric, is a 3, with a chance of a 4 if the scope-and-replay pieces hold up under a Challenge.

**Not checked.** The CROWN-QA appendix. The full text of OpenNovelty and of the three papers I read only as abstracts (2609.36139, 2608.31016, 2602.13855). NoveltyAudit, which returned 404 twice. The READMEs of answerproof, ToolProof and Bernstein beyond their landing pages. Espacenet and Google Patents full text, which need an interactive search. A Challenger with better access should start there.
